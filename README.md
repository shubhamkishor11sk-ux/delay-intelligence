# Delay Intelligence

Regional operations analytics for a logistics network, using synthetic shipment data.
Everything here runs offline with pandas, NumPy, scikit-learn and joblib.

## How to run

Run from the repository root, in this order:

1. `pip install -r requirements.txt`
2. `python generate_data.py`
3. `python part_a_clean_eda.py`
4. `python part_b_regression.py`
5. `python part_c_classification.py`
6. `python part_d_segments.py`
7. `python package_model.py`
8. `python predict_delay.py`

`models/delay_model.pkl` is regenerated by running `python package_model.py`. The
committed pickle loads reliably only on the scikit-learn version pinned in
`requirements.txt`; if your installed versions differ, re-run `package_model.py`
instead of loading the committed pickle.


## Data findings

All numbers below are copied from `outputs/part_a_delay_by_group.csv`,
`outputs/part_a_correlations.csv` and `outputs/part_a_checks.csv`.

1. **Express parcels are delayed most often.** Express has a delay rate of 0.4897
   (119 of 243 parcels), against 0.2056 for Standard and 0.0857 for Economy.

2. **Delay rate rises sharply with hub tier.** It goes from 0.0400 in Tier 1 to
   0.2150 in Tier 2 and 0.4400 in Tier 3.

3. **Delay rate rises with distance.** It is 0.0219 for 0-200 km, 0.1368 for
   201-400 km, 0.4364 for 401-600 km and 0.8523 for 601+ km.

4. **The three columns most correlated with delivery_hours** (ranked by absolute
   value, excluding delivery_hours itself) are `promised_hours` (0.8372),
   `distance_km` (0.4013) and `n_stops` (0.3247).

5. **Why declared_value_inr was log-transformed.** The filled declared value is
   right-skewed, with a mean of INR 1,533.05 well above its median of INR 1,121.50,
   while after the natural log the mean (7.0280) and median (7.0220) are nearly equal,
   so the transformed feature is roughly symmetric.

## Model results (regression)

All numbers come from the `outputs/part_b_*.csv` files.

- **Baseline.** Predicting the training mean gives R² = -0.0212. This means
  the baseline explains almost none of the variation in delivery hours,
  so it is the floor every model must beat.
- **Best model on validation.** Linear regression model won with a validation RMSE of
  4.8912 hours (R² = 0.9313), so its typical error is about 4.8912 hours.
- **Decision-tree depth.** Depths [1,2] are high-bias: train RMSE are 11.6334 and 9.3687 and
  validation RMSE are 12.1324 and 10.4114 are both high with a small gap. Depths [12,None] are
  high-variance: train RMSE falls to 1.3613 and 0.0000 while validation RMSE is 7.4173 and 7.7890,
  a gaps of 6.056 and 7.7890. Depth [6] had the lowest validation RMSE (6.2796).
- **Learning rate.** 0.10 was best (validation RMSE 4.9165,R² 0.9306). At 0.01 the
  model learns too slowly in 100 trees and underfits (RMSE 10.0018, R² 0.7127). At 1.0 each
  tree overshoots the correction and the fit becomes unstable (RMSE 7.3701, R² 0.8440).
- **Cross-validation.** 5-fold CV gave linear regression mean R² 0.9261 (std 0.0048)
  and gradient boosting mean R² 0.9352 (std 0.0044). This disagrees with the validation ranking, I
  would ship gradient boosting model because CV averages over all 1020 training-plus-validation rows 
  instead of one 180-row split, gradient boosting has higher mean R² and more stable scores across folds and validation RMSE is only about 0.02 hours behind a gap   small enough to overcome easily.
- **Final test score.** Gradient boosting scored RMSE 4.9165 hours on the 180 test 
  rows, scored once.


## Model results (classification)

All numbers come from the `outputs/part_c_*.csv` files.

- **Blind baseline.** Predicting "not delayed" for every parcel gives accuracy
  0.7667 but recall 0 (0 of 42 delayed parcels caught), so it is useless for
  catching delays even though its accuracy looks high.
- **Noise columns.** The five bookkeeping columns hold a combined 0.2245 of the
  forest's importance. Their F-score ranks are [8,12,14,16,17], so all five
  fall outside the top 7.
- **Feature count (k).** Validation F1 for k = 5, 7, 10, 12, 17 is 0.6988,0.8333,
  0.7857.0.8049,0.7848. The value increases till k=7 then decreases,so extra columns
  add noise. The brief fixes k = 7 which is used from here on.
- **Tuning.** Grid search reached validation F1 0.7750, randomized search 0.7848
  and the untuned forest on the same 7 columns 0.8333. Tuning did not beat the
  untuned forest. With only 180 validation rows (42 delayed), differences of
  this size are within noise, so tuning on one small validation set is unreliable.
  The chosen configuration is default_forest_same_7_features.
- **Alert threshold.** The threshold is 0.5, the highest with validation recall
  at least 0.80 (recall 0.8333, precision 0.7955). At 0.6 recall falls to 0.7619 
  while precision rises to 0.8205. Lower thresholds raise more unnecessary calls
  (9 at the chosen one) but miss fewer delayed parcels, which is the cheaper error.
- **Test result.** At that threshold the final model scored precision 0.6889,
  recall 0.7561 and F1 0.7209 on the 180 test rows, catching 31 of 41 delayed
  parcels. Validation recall was 0.8333. Test recall is lower and falls below the
  0.80 target, so the validation recall of 0.8333 was somewhat optimistic: the
  threshold was chosen on validation rows, so its recall there is biased upwards,
  while the test rows were never used for any choice. The test set has
  only 41 delayed parcels, so each one moves recall by about 0.024 and the gap
  (about 0.077, roughly three parcels) is partly sampling noise. I would treat
  the true recall as somewhere around 0.76 to 0.83 rather than claim 0.80 is
  guaranteed.

## Operations recommendation

**Escalate segment.** The hubs in the `Escalate` segment are HUB13 (Nagpur),
HUB15 (Kochi), HUB17 (Patna), HUB18 (Ranchi), HUB19 (Guwahati), HUB20 (Raipur),
HUB21 (Bhubaneswar), HUB23 (Siliguri) and HUB24 (Agartala), with delay rates of
0.4600, 0.3600, 0.4600, 0.3800, 0.6200, 0.4800, 0.4000, 0.4400 and 0.5400
respectively. HUB19 (Guwahati) is the worst at 0.6200.

**Tier labels that mislead.** `tier_vs_segment_mismatch.csv` lists four hubs:
HUB07 (Kolkata, Tier 1, segment Watch, delay rate 0.1400), HUB13 (Nagpur, Tier 2,
segment Escalate, delay rate 0.4600), HUB15 (Kochi, Tier 2, segment Escalate, delay
rate 0.3600) and HUB22 (Dehradun, Tier 3, segment Watch, delay rate 0.2000). For
these hubs the tier label does not describe how the hub actually behaves, so
planners should use the segment, not the tier, when choosing a playbook. HUB07 is
labelled Tier 1 (expected Standard) but is delayed far more often than a Tier 1
hub, so it should get the Watch playbook. HUB22 is labelled Tier 3 (expected
Escalate) but behaves like a Watch hub, so it should get the Watch playbook rather
than the heaviest one. HUB13 and HUB15 are labelled Tier 2 (expected Watch) but
behave like stretched hubs, so they should get the Escalate playbook. Tier labels
remain usable for the other 20 hubs, whose segment matches their tier.

**Alert threshold.** Flag a parcel when its delay probability is at least 0.5,
the highest threshold with validation recall of at least 0.80. On the 180 test rows
(`part_c_final_test.csv`), of every 10 flagged parcels about 7 are
really delayed (precision 0.6889), and of every 10 delayed parcels about
8 are caught (recall 0.7561).Test recall fell short of the 0.80 target, so the real 
catch rate may be closer to 7 or 8 of every 10 than to 8 or more.

**New parcels.** Of the 48 new parcels, 8 were flagged, and 6 of those need a
hub-manager escalation (flagged and in an `Escalate` hub). The other 2 flagged
parcels get a proactive customer call.

**Why K = 3.** `part_d_separation.csv` shows across-to-within ratios of 2.8940, 5.3928,
6.3047 and 6.8866 for K = 2 to 5 (cluster sizes 15-9, 9-8-7, 8-7-5-4 and 8-7-4-3-2).
The ratio rises with every added cluster, with the biggest jump from K = 2 to K = 3
(from 2.8940 to 5.3928) and much smaller gains after that (about 0.91 from K = 3 to 4
and about 0.58 from K = 4 to 5). A higher K always tends to raise this ratio, because
clusters get smaller and tighter, so the ratio alone cannot choose K. I kept K = 3
because operations runs exactly three playbooks, and at K = 3 the clusters are
balanced (9-8-7) and already well separated, with hubs in different clusters about
5.4 times farther apart on average than hubs in the same cluster. Going to K = 4 or 5
only splits off small clusters of 2 to 5 hubs, which are too small to run as
separate playbooks.


## Production plan

**(a) Production-readiness mapping**

| Characteristic | Item in this repository |
|---|---|
| Accuracy | `part_c_final_test.csv`: scored once on 180 test rows, accuracy 0.8667, precision 0.6889, recall 0.7561 (below the 0.80 target); validation metrics per threshold in `part_c_thresholds.csv`; stratified split |
| Reliability | `clean_shipments` handles duplicates, casing, blanks and the 999 kg sentinel; stored medians fill gaps, and all 48 new parcels were scored with no missing feature values |
| Reproducibility | Fixed `random_state` values, pinned `requirements.txt`, committed `generate_data.py`, scripts run in a fixed order, and two runs of `predict_delay.py` give byte-identical output |
| Performance (speed) | Scoring 48 parcels takes about 1.6 seconds with the saved model; no retraining at prediction time |
| Maintainability | Shared code in `src/shipment_prep.py`, one script per Part, configuration in CSV files |
| Consistency | The same `clean_shipments` and `build_features` run in training and in `predict_delay.py`; `feature_order.csv` fixes the column order; `impute_values.csv` stores training medians |
| Monitorability | `new_shipment_actions.csv` records every probability and flag per parcel; delivered outcomes can be joined to it later to track flag rates and recall |

**(b) Quantities to monitor**

| Quantity | Numeric trigger |
|---|---|
| Weekly delayed share of delivered parcels | Investigate if it differs by more than 10 percentage points from the historical 0.2317 (outside 0.1317 to 0.3317) for two weeks in a row, outside a sale window |
| Weekly share of parcels flagged | Investigate if it leaves 0.15 to 0.35 (the share flagged on the test rows was 0.25, which is 45 of 180) |
| Recall on parcels whose outcome is now known | Retrain if recall falls below 0.70 over the last 4 weeks, provided at least 40 delayed parcels are in the window (target was 0.80; test recall was 0.7561) |

**(c) Retraining and drift.** Retrain every quarter, because the history covers only
90 days and new delivered parcels will add delayed cases to learn from, and sooner
if any trigger above fires. Use the latest delivered parcels, keep `sale_event_flag`
as a feature and include sale parcels so the model learns the sale effect, then
re-run `part_d_segments.py` and `package_model.py` and re-check the alert
threshold on fresh validation data. The 15 to 28 September sale window
(`sale_event_flag`) raises delivery hours, so compare it against the same kind of
event (the previous sale's weeks), not against ordinary weeks, and pause the
ordinary-week delayed-share trigger during the window; otherwise the monitors would
raise false alarms during every sale. Data drift here would look like: a hub's mean
distance or mean stops moving by more than 15% from its row in `hub_profile.csv`,
new service types or hubs, a change in the COD share or parcel weights, a hub
moving to another segment, or a rising share of missing values, any of which makes
the stored medians in `impute_values.csv` and the segments out of date.

## Limitations

1. **Synthetic data.** All shipments are produced by a generator, with a linear
   structure and noise of 4 hours, so real performance would differ, and the brand is
   only the setting.
2. **Few delayed parcels in the test set.** The test set has only 41 delayed parcels,
   so each one moves recall by about 0.024. Test recall (0.7561) versus validation
   recall (0.8333) differ by 0.0772, which is partly sampling noise.
3. **Threshold tuned on one validation split.** The threshold of 0.5 was chosen on 180 rows
   (42 delayed), so it may not give recall of 0.80 on new data.
4. **Segments from 24 hubs.** K-Means on 24 points is sensitive to the hub profile
   and the chosen K, and the hub profiles are based on about 50 parcels each.
5. **One time period.** The data covers 90 days, so seasonality beyond the sale
   window is not captured.
